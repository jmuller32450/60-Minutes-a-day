60 minutes a day

Australia's physical activity guidelines for young people

You need at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity every day, and it can be split up across the day. Does what you do count? Your heart rate will tell you.

Light

Easy. Still good for you (aim for a few hours a day), but it doesn't count towards your 60 minutes.

Moderate

You can talk, but you can't sing.

Vigorous

You can only say a few words at a time. Aim for this on at least 3 days a week.

Taking your pulse

  • Put two fingers (not your thumb) on the inside of your wrist, or beside your windpipe under your jaw.
  • Count the beats for 15 seconds, then × 4. That's your beats per minute (bpm).
  • Count straight away when you stop. Your heart starts slowing down the second you finish.
  • Rest for 1–2 minutes between movements so your heart rate settles.
Stay safe: warm up with your teacher first. Stop and tell your teacher if you feel dizzy, sick or have chest pain. If you have asthma or a heart condition, check with your teacher before you start.

For teachers

Guidelines: Australian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for Children and Young People (5–17 years), Australian Government Department of Health. Physical activity: at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day (more is better, and it can be accumulated); vigorous activities and muscle- and bone-strengthening activities on at least 3 days a week; several hours of light activity a day.

Heart rate zones and talk test: not part of the Australian guidelines, which only describe moderate to vigorous activity as activity "that makes the heart beat faster". Zones use the US CDC target heart rate method: estimated maximum heart rate = 220 − age (207 bpm at age 13); moderate ≈ 64–76% (132–158 bpm), vigorous ≈ 77–93% (159–193 bpm). The talk test (talk but not sing = moderate; only a few words = vigorous) is also from the CDC. 220 − age is a rough estimate, especially for young people, so treat the zones as a guide.
